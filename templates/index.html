Understanding the Medical Terms

Not familiar with these medical values? Here's what they mean.

Bilirubin

Bilirubin is a yellow substance produced when old red blood cells break down. The liver processes bilirubin and helps remove it from the body.

Why included? Abnormal bilirubin levels can be associated with problems involving liver function or bile flow.

ALT — Alamine Aminotransferase

ALT is an enzyme found mainly in the liver. When liver cells are damaged, ALT can enter the bloodstream.

Why included? It is commonly used as an indicator of liver-cell injury.

AST — Aspartate Aminotransferase

AST is an enzyme found in the liver and several other tissues. Changes in AST levels can occur when cells are damaged.

Why included? It provides additional information about the patient's biochemical profile.

Alkaline Phosphatase

Alkaline phosphatase is an enzyme associated with the liver, bile ducts, and bones.

Why included? Abnormal levels can sometimes be associated with problems involving bile flow or other conditions.

Albumin

Albumin is a protein produced mainly by the liver. It helps maintain fluid balance in the bloodstream.

Why included? Albumin levels can provide information about the patient's protein and liver-related status.

Total Proteins

Total protein measures the major proteins present in the blood, including albumin and globulins.

Why included? It provides information about the patient's overall protein profile.

Albumin / Globulin Ratio

The A/G ratio compares the amount of albumin with globulins in the blood.

Why included? Changes in the ratio can provide additional information about the patient's biochemical profile.

Age

The patient's age in years.

Why included? Age is included as a demographic feature used by the machine learning model.

Gender

The patient's reported gender used as a demographic feature in the dataset.

Why included? Gender is included as a patient-level feature used by the model.
These explanations are provided for educational purposes and are not medical advice.
